Your salary offer usually shows your **gross pay**: the amount before income tax and other deductions. Your **take-home pay** is what remains after those deductions are taken out.

## Start with gross pay

Gross pay is your salary before deductions. If you are paid monthly, begin with your annual salary and divide it by twelve. Your payslip may also show allowances, bonuses or employer contributions separately, so check which figures are included in the headline amount.

## Subtract taxes and contributions

Income tax depends on where you live, how much you earn and which tax rules apply. Payroll deductions can also include social insurance, pension contributions or workplace benefits. These amounts may change when your salary, tax status or local rules change.

For a useful estimate, list each deduction separately rather than applying one guessed percentage to your whole salary. A calculator can help compare scenarios, but your actual payslip is the best source for your final net pay.

## Compare the result with your payslip

Check the pay period, taxable income and deductions on your payslip. If the estimate and payslip differ, look for items such as pension contributions, tax credits, insurance or one-time adjustments. Ask your payroll team about entries you do not recognise.
